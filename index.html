    01How it works · The lid

    How it works

    Sunlight from above, nutrients from below

    In simple terms

    Phytoplankton need two main inputs: sunlight from above and food (nutrients) from below. Stratification, the divide between layers of water, means warm water floats on top of cold water, as if it were a lid. Salt matters too: melting ice is fresh, so it is lighter than seawater and forms a lid of its own. Near the tropics, this lid is thicker, so less food can rise to the surface, whereas in the Arctic, melting ice lets in light. Notwithstanding, volcanoes can produce similar, and even harsher, effects, so they have to be separated from the longer-term trend.

    Phytoplankton: from the Greek phytón, ‘plant’, and planktós, ‘wandering’. The drifting plants.

    These plants depend on a single arrangement: sunlight from above, nutrients from below, and a layer of warm water between them that acts as a lid. Since 2002, that lid has tightened over the Pacific, where chlorophyll has fallen 4.3 per cent per decade, and lifted in the Arctic, where it has risen 16.4 per cent. However, a volcano can rupture it in a week.

    The trend detector that we built, on 23 years of MODIS-Aqua ocean colour, operates on the basis of four fundamental questions: what is changing, where, by how much, and whether that change is a trend or an anomaly attributable to a volcanic eruption, based on NASA’s Multi-Satellite Volcanic SO₂ database.

    02How it works · Method

    Trend Isolation

    What remains when the seasons are removed?

    Figure IIINorth Pacific Gyre chlorophyll, 2002–2025. Raw: every winter peak and summer low. Seasons removed: each month against its own average (faint line), and the yearly means used for the trend (dots).

    Log scale. Chlorophyll spans orders of magnitude, from 0.04 mg/m³ in the gyre to over 3 in an Arctic bloom, so each series was log-transformed. A change from 0.04 to 0.08 then counts the same as one from 2 to 4: a doubling.

    Seasons. Spring blooms and winter lows repeat every year, so they say nothing about change. We subtracted each calendar month’s average from that month, leaving only how unusual each month was, then averaged those into one value per year.

    Tests. The Mann-Kendall test seeks to find out whether there is a monotonic upward or downward trend in a variable, by comparing every pair of years and counting how often the later year is higher. Should rises far outnumber falls, the trend is unlikely to be dictated by chance alone. Sen’s slope takes the median of every year-to-year slope, so single extreme years, such as volcanic bloom events, cannot drag the rate.

    S = Σi<j sign(yj − yi)   ·   β = median[ (yj − yi) / (j − i) ]

    S counts rises minus falls across every pair of years; β is Sen’s slope. Its 95% interval comes from the ranks of the pairwise slopes, and the p-value from the normal approximation to S, with ties corrected.

    03How it works · Limits

    Limitations

    Where could this mislead?

    Record length. Twenty-three years is short for chlorophyll, which swings with El Niño from year to year. Studies suggest 30 to 40 years are needed to separate a climate trend from this natural variability, so the gyre decline is early evidence; an expanded record would make it more reliable.

    Arctic satellite bias. Arctic rivers carry coloured dissolved organic matter, which absorbs light much like chlorophyll. In simple terms, the satellite can mistake river water for plants and overestimate chlorophyll near the coast.

    01Trends · Tropics

    The Tightening Lid

    Why is the North Pacific Gyre losing its plants?

    Figure IVGyre chlorophyll, yearly, vs normal ·

    Figure VGyre sea surface temperature, yearly, vs normal ·

    Between 2003 and 2025, the surface of the North Pacific Gyre warmed by 0.30 °C per decade (p = 0.006), while its chlorophyll fell by 4.3 per cent per decade (p = 0.017). A warmer surface is lighter, so it resists mixing with the cold, nutrient-rich water beneath it, and less food reaches the surface.

    Year to year, the two move only loosely together (r = −0.33, not significant), as El Niño and eddies add noise. The link shows in the long-run trends, not in every single year.

    03Trends · Volcanoes

    The Volcanic Rupture

    Can an eruption be told apart from a trend?

    Volcanic ash carries iron, the missing ingredient across much of the ocean, while lava entering the sea can lift deep, nutrient-rich water to the surface. For each eruption, we compared chlorophyll in the weeks afterwards with the same weeks in every other year, a placebo test borrowed from event studies in finance. Kīlauea and Tonga were the highest of any year; Kasatochi was second; Raikoke, fifth. Together, p ≈ 0.008.

    z = (log10C − μweek) / σweek   ·   p = (k + 1) / (n + 1)

    μ and σ are the mean and spread for the same 8-day period in all other years. The test-window mean of z is ranked against the same window in each of the n other years; k is the number of years that scored as high or higher.
